CNN explorer, training

The same toy network as the other two pages, now learning. Real MNIST digits, shrunk to 16×16, come in one at a time: the network guesses, the loss says how wrong the guess was, the error flows back through the layers to give every weight a gradient, and every weight moves a little the other way. That is training; the rest is repetition. Step through one digit below, then play to watch the weights change over thousands. Being a toy of 12 kernels on 16×16 pixels, it reaches about 80 to 85% on digits it has never seen after a few passes, well short of a real LeNet's 99%, but every mechanism is the real one.

One step

digit → forward → loss → backward → update

Step runs one phase at a time; Next digit finishes this digit and brings the next one in. While Many steps plays, this band stays still; pause to see the current digit. Forward is what the other pages show. Backward is new: the gradient of the loss starts at the class scores as p − y and flows back through the same shapes, and each weight's gradient is what came forward times what came back at that weight. Update nudges every weight by learning rate × gradient, the other way. Hover any cell or weight for its arithmetic.

Many steps

play · curves · kernels over time · held-out accuracy

Play repeats the step for digit after digit. The loss curve shows the loss per digit, which is noisy, and its running average. Accuracy is measured two ways: on the last 200 training digits, and every 200 digits on 1,000 held-out test digits the network never trains on. The strip of kernels shows layer 1 at fixed milestones: with random initialisation it has to discover edges on its own; "start from edge detectors" begins from the hand-picked ones instead. Expect a plateau first: for several hundred digits the loss sits near 2.3, which is −log(1/10), a coin toss over ten classes, because eight averages of random kernels barely separate the digits. Then it drops. From the edge detectors it drops at once.
