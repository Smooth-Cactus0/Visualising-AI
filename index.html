Visualising AI

Interactive explorers for deep-learning architectures. Each one takes the boxes of an architecture diagram and shows what they compute: every cell, kernel, weight and gradient can be hovered, and the arithmetic is written out. The networks are small on purpose, so that every number fits on screen.

The convolutional network is built, and the Vision Transformer has its explorer. Next: the Vision Transformer's training page, then U-Net, CoAtNet and ConvNeXt.

01

Convolutional network

3 pages

Small learned filters slide over the image. Stacked, with pooling in between, they turn pixels into edges, edges into shapes, and shapes into a class.

It asks, at every position: does the pattern I learned appear here? Each layer sees a slightly wider patch than the one before, so the view grows a few pixels at a time.

02

Vision Transformer (ViT)

1 page

Cut the image into patches, turn each patch into a vector called a token, and let every token look at every other one through attention.

Where a convolution's view grows a few pixels per layer, attention sees the whole image from the first layer and learns where to look. The price: its cost grows with the square of the number of patches, which is why patches are large.

03

U-Net, encoder and decoder

planned

An encoder shrinks the image into features, a decoder grows them back to full size, and skip connections hand the fine detail straight across.

The CNN here ends in 10 numbers for the whole image. A U-Net ends in an image: a label for every pixel, which is segmentation. The question changes from "what is this?" to "which pixels are it?".

What the page will show
  • The contracting path: the convolutions and pooling of the CNN pages, adding channels as the map shrinks.
  • Up-sampling by transposed convolution, the mirror image of a strided one.
  • Skip connections joined as extra channels, and what the output loses without them.
  • One output pixel's receptive field, reaching down through the bottleneck and across the skips.

Builds on the CNN pages: convolution, pooling, receptive field.

Convolution stages (C) while the map is large, attention stages (T) once it is small. Yellow: what one output reads.
04

CoAtNet, convolution then attention

planned

Convolution stages first, attention stages after: cheap local detail while the map is large, then global context once it is small.

It asks where in the network to stop looking locally and start looking everywhere, and what that switch costs. Its diagram is the one that started this project.

What the page will show
  • MBConv stages: expand the channels, a depthwise convolution, squeeze and excite, project back.
  • The switch from local to global, with the receptive field before and after it.
  • Attention with a relative position bias, which keeps a little of convolution's sense of neighbourhood.
  • The cost of each stage, which explains the order.

05

ConvNeXt, a modernised CNN

planned

A plain convolutional network, rebuilt step by step with the design choices that made transformers work, until it matches them.

It asks whether attention was the point, or the recipe around it. Each change is small and can be switched on and off.

What the page will show
  • The patchify stem: a 4×4 convolution with stride 4, the ViT patch embedding again.
  • 7×7 depthwise convolution: one slice per channel and no sum across channels, against the 3×3×C kernel of the CNN pages.
  • 1×1 convolutions as the channel-mixing step, in an inverted bottleneck that widens four times and narrows back.
  • LayerNorm, GELU and the residual connection, and what each changes.
