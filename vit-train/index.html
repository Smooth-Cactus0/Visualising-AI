ViT training

The toy Vision Transformer of the explorer, now learning. Real MNIST digits shrunk to 16×16 come in one at a time; the loss says how wrong the guess was, and the error flows back from the loss through the classifier head, the MLP, attention and the patch embedding, giving every weight a gradient. One step

digit → forward → backward, a stage at a time → update

The ViT is laid out as on the explorer, token i on row i, with three rows: forward (activations, and the weights where they act), backward (the gradient of each, under it) and update (w′ = w − learning rate × gradient, under each weight). Step runs one phase. Backward starts at the class scores as p − y and flows right to left, a stage per step: the classifier head, the MLP, attention, the patch embedding; each step frames in green what it computed. Through attention the error flows the other way round from the forward pass: a token's output read a row of the attention map, so the gradient of a token's key and value collects from a column, every query that read it, in proportion. A weight's gradient is what came forward into it times what came back out of it, summed over the 16 tokens when the 16 tokens share the weight. Next digit finishes this digit and brings the next. While Many steps plays, this band follows the training.

Many steps

play · curves · attention and position over training

Play repeats the step for digit after digit, with SGD. The loss curve shows the loss per digit and its running average; accuracy is measured on the last 200 training digits and, every 500 digits, on the 1,000 held-out digits the network never trains on. This transformer is sensitive to the learning rate: at 0.1 and above it learns badly or not at all (measured on the 3,000 digits, ten passes: 0.1 reached 29.7%, 0.3 diverged at 15.6%), where the CNN page trains well at 0.1. Each attention map is 16 × 16: row i is where token i looks. The similarity picture puts, at each patch slot, a 4 × 4 map of how alike that slot's row of the position table is to every other slot's (orange alike, blue opposite). In the ViT paper, trained on millions of images, these maps form a 2D grid: neighbours alike. Here, on 3,000 digits, no grid forms (measured on the explorer's shipped weights and on Adam-trained weights: neighbouring slots are no more alike than distant ones), yet the model leans on position: switched off, the held-out accuracy of the explorer's shipped weights (one run) falls from 82% to 34%. It uses each row as a label for its slot, not as a map.

CNN against ViT

held-out accuracy after 30,000 steps, by number of training digits

Both toys trained on the same few digits, 30,000 SGD steps each, each at its own learning rate (CNN 0.1, the CNN training page's default; ViT 0.003, the explorer's). A convolution only combines neighbouring pixels, so a CNN starts out knowing that nearby pixels belong together; attention can join any two patches, so a ViT has to learn locality from the examples. With few examples that costs it a lot. Each dot is one run with its own seed; the lines join the averages.
